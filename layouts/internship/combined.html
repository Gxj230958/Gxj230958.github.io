Where does the training gain come from?

A gated expert sends each input through two projections. Their gradients return to the same input, but preparing the two matrix products can take longer than computing them. On Ascend 910B, grouping the products cuts complete Qwen3 expert-block time by 7.95–37.20% with the forward pass, routing and weight-gradient path held fixed.

The integration study measured the benefit of changing the entire expert path in verl. The matched study then kept the packed forward projection and physical weight-gradient layout in both arms and varied only the input-gradient schedule. The two comparisons have different denominators. Each percentage is relative to its own baseline.

Two products share a launch and keep their rounding points.

A matrix multiplication writes a BF16 result for each of the gate and up branches. Those two rounded results are added to form the input gradient. Moving the addition inside a single wide dot product can change that gradient. The implementation assigns two internal groups to each expert, so one native grouped matrix multiplication still writes both BF16 results.

Pack the gradients

For an expert receiving n routed rows, store its n gate rows followed by its n up rows. Each group receives a count of n.

View the weights

Expose the existing packed weights as 2E groups for E experts. One native call computes the two products for each expert.

Combine the pair

Read both BF16 products for the same token, widen them for addition, and write the input gradient in BF16.

branch_weight = weight.transpose(1, 2).view(2 * e, i, h)
branches = torch_npu.npu_grouped_matmul(
    [packed], [branch_weight], bias=None, group_list=groups,
    split_item=2, group_type=0, group_list_type=1,
)[0]

Four slicing tasks dominate the short block.

In the 128-row Qwen3 hotspot, slicing the two branches consumes 1.351 ms in the matched baseline. Packing and combining take 0.162 ms in the grouped arm. The native matrix products themselves take 0.066 ms and 0.060 ms, respectively. Most of the observed saving comes from preparing the operands.

When grouping loses

A pretrained Mixtral layer with its learned top-2 router supplies a test at a different expert width. At 32,768 text rows, packing and combining still take less time than the baseline slices, but native matrix-multiplication time increases by 10.624 ms in the device trace. The complete block runs 1.20% slower. These row counts aggregate tokens from text windows before expert routing.

Matched evaluation and routing controls

From one expert block to an actor update.

The full layout patch reduced median update time from 32.730 s to 31.259 s on the 48-layer Qwen3 actor across 16 NPUs. A separate profile counted 576 fewer grouped-matmul layout materializations per rank. To isolate dX, the matched experiment replayed a fixed PPO batch on a 24-layer, 15.577B-parameter actor across eight NPUs. Median time fell in each independent session, with exact all-rank agreement in the checked training state.

Training quality under native execution

The longer training runs evaluated a configuration that also enabled a communication cast/scale hook. Two-layer runs covered five seeds and 1,000 updates. The 48-layer runs covered three seeds and 200 updates. For the full model, the largest one-sided 95% upper bound on the final candidate-minus-reference negative log-likelihood across three splits was 0.001080 nats/token, below the prespecified 0.005 limit. These measurements apply to the combined configuration. Training snapshots and evaluation data

When a local saving reaches the collective

A separate 16-NPU experiment varied when rank 0 reached a collective on a 623-million-element gradient bucket. Fusing rank 0’s cast and divide roughly halved its preparation time in both regimes. The difference in completion-time savings between late and early arrival was 3.787 ms (95% interval 3.740–3.838 ms).

Scope of the evidence

The measured speedups apply to the Ascend 910B stack. Mixtral covers one pretrained expert layer. The distributed timing measures deterministic, fixed-batch PPO actor updates with synthetic advantages. End-to-end rollout throughput and performance on other accelerators remain open. Under the original full-model native runtime, strict comparisons exceeded their tolerances for 91 normalization gradients and two parameter updates. The deterministic replay and the longer-run quality tests answer different numerical questions.
